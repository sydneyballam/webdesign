About Us

The mission of the Willow Creek Fire Safe Council is to reduce wildfire risk and increase survivability by implementing fuels reduction projects and encouraging residents of the greater Willow Creek area to make their homes, neighborhoods and community fire safe.

Willow Creek Fire Safe Council is incorporated as a nonprofit public benefit corporation in the State of California. Willow Creek Fire Safe Council is exempt from federal income taxes under IRC Section 501(c)(3) as a public charity.

The primary objectives of the Willow Creek Fire Safe Council are:

  1. AIncrease community awareness and understanding of both the benefits and threats of wildland fire
  2. BEducate residents on ways to protect their lives and property from loss to catastrophic wildland fire
  3. CGenerate individual and community action to reduce wildfire hazards and plan for emergency response
  4. DReduce wildfire hazards by removing flammable vegetation around homes and evacuation routes
  5. EEnhance communication between the public and federal, state, county and local agencies to identify and prioritize hazard reduction projects and co-ordinate community planning
  6. FDevelop and monitor a Community Wildfire Protection Plan for the Willow Creek area to address hazard mitigation, community preparedness, structure protection and wildfire response
  7. GEngage the youth of our communities in fire prevention by providing activities and educational opportunities related to fire safety and protecting natural resources

Community Chipping Program

The Willow Creek Fire Safe Council (WCFSC) provides free chipping services for homeowners and residents of the Willow Creek area. Chipping the woody materials produced during tree pruning and fuel reduction activities around homes and along road frontage is an alternative to burning or hauling. The chips are left in a pile on-site, and provide a great source of mulch and ground cover to prevent erosion and improve soil moisture and health. Please follow these instructions to ensure a safe and effective chipper day.

What Can and Cannot be Chipped?

  • Yes!Collect and pile woody material before scheduled chipping day
  • Yes!Designate a place for chipped material within 5ft. of where chipper will be parked
  • Yes!Stack material in rows no greater than 5' tall, 10' wide, and 20' long
  • Yes!Limbs no greater than 6" in diameter and 3-10' long
  • Yes!Neatly stacked piles with cut ends of branches facing toward the street, driveway, or turnout
  • NoLimbs over 6" in diameter and shorter than 3' or longer than 10' (it's a safety hazard!)
  • NoLeaves or lawn clippings
  • NoRoots, root wads, stumps, or soil
  • NoLumber, fence posts, gravel, nails, trash, pressure treated wood
  • NoPlants such as poison oak or oleander (poisonous), blackberry brambles or roses (spiny), or scotch broom (invasive and spreads easily)
  • NoBay laurel, tan oak, or rhododendron infected (or suspected to be) with Sudden Oak Death
  • NoCommercial timber slash, orchard prunings, or materials left by tree companies

2025 PG&E Western Willow Creek Risk Reduction Project

Brannan Mountain Roadside Fuels Reduction

This project focused on roadside fuel reduction on private property along Brannan Mountain Road, treating within 500 feet of PG&E infrastructure and driveways adjacent to powerlines.

The resulting shaded fuel breaks protect PG&E electrical assets and reduces the risk of wildfire ignition and spreading into the communities and households that PG&E serves. The neighborhood has improved ingress and egress for residents and emergency services along Brannan Mountain Road, which is a vital secondary evacuation route for Eastern Humboldt residents.

Greater Willow Creek Wildfire Resilience project

This project removes dangerous hazardous fuels on private property, focusing on roadsides and within 100 feet of homes to create defensible space. This important work makes our community more safe by creating safe ingress/egress and protecting residents.

The next portion of this project will focus on completing Home Risk Assessments for homeowners. Homeowners can learn how to harden their homes against catastrophic wildfire, register water sources with the Blue Dot Program, and get address signs that will aid emergency services.

A Regional Partnership

This multi-year project was designed and is being implemented in partnership with Humboldt County Resource Conservation District, Trinity County Resource Conservation District, and Willow Creek Volunteer Fire Department.
