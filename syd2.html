Our mission

To reduce wildfire risk and increase survivability.

We do that by implementing fuels reduction projects and encouraging residents of the Willow Creek area to make their homes, neighborhoods and communities fire safe.

Willow Creek Fire Safe Council is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation and a 501(c)(3) public charity.

What we work toward

  1. AIncrease community awareness and understanding of both the benefits and threats of wildland fire
  2. BEducate residents on ways to protect their lives and property from loss to catastrophic wildland fire
  3. CGenerate individual and community action to reduce wildfire hazards and plan for emergency response
  4. DReduce wildfire hazards by removing flammable vegetation around homes and evacuation routes
  5. EEnhance communication between the public and federal, state, county and local agencies to identify and prioritize hazard reduction projects and co-ordinate community planning
  6. FDevelop and monitor a Community Wildfire Protection Plan for the Willow Creek area to address hazard mitigation, community preparedness, structure protection and wildfire response
  7. GEngage the youth of our communities in fire prevention by providing activities and educational opportunities related to fire safety and protecting natural resources

Projects on the ground

Roadsides cleared, evacuation routes opened

2025 PG&E Western Willow Creek Risk Reduction Project

Brannan Mountain Roadside Fuels Reduction

We treated roadside fuels on private property along Brannan Mountain Road, within 500 feet of PG&E infrastructure and driveways next to powerlines.

The shaded fuel breaks protect electrical assets and lower the risk of an ignition spreading into the homes PG&E serves. Residents and emergency crews now have better ingress and egress on a vital secondary evacuation route for Eastern Humboldt.

Multi-year regional partnership

Greater Willow Creek Wildfire Resilience

Crews remove hazardous fuels on private property, focusing on roadsides and the 100 feet around homes. That creates defensible space and safer ways in and out.

The next phase is free home risk assessments. You learn how to harden your home, register water sources with the Blue Dot Program, and get an address sign that helps emergency services.

Built with Humboldt County Resource Conservation District, Trinity County Resource Conservation District, and Willow Creek Volunteer Fire Department.
