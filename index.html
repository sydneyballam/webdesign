Starting out in Humboldt County

The usual path from idea to open for business.

1. Choose your name

Pick a name that fits you, and check that no one else nearby is already using it.

2. Register the name

If your business name doesn't include your own surname, the county asks for a fictitious business name filing.

3. Get your county license

Businesses outside city limits apply through the county. I'll help you find the right type and fill in the form.

4. Check state and tax questions

I'll point you to the right offices so you can ask the right people the right questions.

5. Get online

A domain name, a business email address, a simple website, and a booking link.

6. Welcome your first customers

A Facebook page and a few simple posts, so your neighbors know you're open.

I'm a guide, not a lawyer or an accountant. I'll help you find the right offices and fill out the forms. Rules and fees change, so we'll check the official sources together.

Learn something

A few handy things worth knowing.

What's the difference between CC and BCC?

To is the main recipient.

CC sends a copy, and everyone can see each other's addresses.

BCC sends a copy and hides the addresses.

When you email a group of customers, use BCC so they don't see each other's addresses.

How to check an email before you click

Look at the sender's actual address, not just the name next to it.

Take a moment with anything urgent, unexpected attachments, or links that don't match who the email says it's from.

Unsure? Pause, and call the company using a number you already have.

Back up your important files

Keep your business files in two places: your computer, and one other place such as a cloud folder or an external drive.

That way you always have a copy. Check now and then that the backup works.
